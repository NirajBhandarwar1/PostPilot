# Style examples

Public posts that did well, kept private. Copy this file to `examples.md` (which is gitignored), or make it with
`python -m postpilot examples fetch`. The writer sees 3 of them per post as examples of structure and rhythm and
must never copy their words or facts. Keep a line with only --- between posts; the first line of a post is its header.

The two posts below are invented, only to show the format. Replace them with real posts you admire.

---
Example Author | 1,200 likes, 85 comments | https://www.linkedin.com/posts/example-1
I stopped trusting my own demo videos.

Last week I ran the same agent on 20 real inputs instead of the 3 I had rehearsed. It failed on 7.

Here is what I changed:

1. I wrote the failures down before touching any code
2. I fixed the cheapest 3 first
3. I re-ran all 20, not just the ones I had fixed

The score went from 13/20 to 18/20 in an afternoon, and I trust that number more than any clip.

What is the first thing you check before you show a demo to anyone?

Example Author Two | 640 likes, 41 comments | https://www.linkedin.com/posts/example-2
Everyone is comparing the two new models. Nobody is checking what each one costs per task.

So I ran 50 identical tasks through both.

Model A: 41 correct, about 4 cents a task.
Model B: 44 correct, about 11 cents a task.

3 more right answers for 2.7x the price. For my use that is a no.

Your numbers will differ, and that is the point: measure your own tasks, not the leaderboard.

Which would you pick, and why?
